Privacy Policy

Local Line for Android (package com.locallinedriver) · Effective 25 September 2026

Local Line (“the app”) is a train-driving game published by Dion (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what information the app handles, why, and what choices you have. The short version: the app sends each finished run to our own server under an anonymous player ID so that the world boards can be scored; it never asks for your name, email address or any account, and the only name it holds is the one you may choose to show on the boards. It also uses Google for advertising, crash reports, optional alerts and in-app purchases. You can delete everything the server holds about you from inside the app.

1. Information the app stores on your device

The app saves your progress in its private storage on your phone so that it is there the next time you play:

  • Run results: scores, stop grades, ratings, streaks, and the logbook of completed runs.
  • Records and mastery grades per line, and which lines you have unlocked.
  • Daily-run results.
  • Settings, such as the graphics level, whether automatic train control is on, and whether alerts are on.
  • Your install ID and player ID (section 2), and the board name you chose, if any.
  • Run reports and usage events that have not yet been sent to our server, so a run driven offline is sent later.
  • Perks earned by watching a rewarded ad: a second attempt at the day's daily run, and a one-day pass on a locked line.

The app opts out of Android's cloud backup, so this data is not copied to your Google account. Uninstalling the app deletes it from the phone; see section 8 for what stays on the server.

2. Information sent to our server

The app talks to a server we run so that every line can have a world board and so that we can see which stations are too hard and which phones run the game badly. The server is hosted on Amazon Web Services in the United States. It receives:

  • Player identity. The first time it runs, the app makes a random install ID and registers it with the server, which answers with a player ID. Neither is tied to a Google account, a phone number, an email address, or the device's advertising ID. If you choose a name for the world boards, that name is stored with your player ID and shown to other players beside your scores. You do not have to pick a name to play.
  • Run reports. Every finished run: the line, direction, train, weather and settings used, when the run started and finished, the stopping and timing error at each station, the points and grade, and whether the run ended in a wreck. Only complete, free runs of a whole line without a retry are placed on the boards; the rest are kept for the difficulty statistics.
  • Device and performance data. Sent with each run report: the phone model, Android version and graphics chip, the app version, how long the scene took to build, and how smoothly it ran. This is how slow phones are found and fixed.
  • Usage events. Small events such as a board being opened, a name being set, or alerts being switched on or off, with your player ID and a random per-session ID.

Board rows show your chosen name, or no name, together with your points, grade and rank. Nothing on the server identifies you beyond the player ID and the name you chose.

3. Alerts (optional)

You can ask to be told when another player beats your best on a line. Nothing is set up unless you say yes, either on the card shown after your first run on a world board or with the ALERTS chip on the title board. When you switch alerts on, the app asks Android for notification permission, asks Firebase Cloud Messaging for a push token, and sends that token and your time zone to our server so that one notification can reach you, at most one a day per line and only in daytime. Switching alerts off, or deleting your data, removes the token from our server and from the phone. Firebase processes the token under the Google Privacy Policy.

4. Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

If the app crashes or stops responding, Firebase Crashlytics sends Google a crash report: the stack trace, the phone model and Android version, a Firebase installation ID, and which line, train and screen the app was on. We use these reports only to find and fix bugs. Firebase Analytics is switched off in the app. Crash reports are kept by Firebase for 90 days and are processed under the Google Privacy Policy.

5. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The free version of the app shows ads served by Google AdMob: a banner strip on the title board, the run board, the logbook and in the cab; an occasional full-screen ad after a run; and rewarded ads that you choose to watch for a perk in the game (a penalty waived, a second attempt at the daily run, a day's pass on a locked line, or keeping a streak through a retry). Rewarded ads are never shown unless you tap to watch one. Buying the ad removal through Google Play turns off the banner and full-screen ads; rewarded ads stay available for you to choose.

To request an ad, Google's Mobile Ads SDK sends Google information about your device, such as its model, operating system, language, IP address (from which an approximate location may be inferred), the app's package name, and the Android advertising ID. Google uses this to select and measure ads, and, where you have allowed it, to personalise them. Google processes this information under the Google Privacy Policy; how Google uses information from apps that use its services is described at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Your choices:

  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the app asks for your consent before requesting any ad, using Google's consent form, and you can change your choice at any time from the PRIVACY chip on the title board. Declining leaves you with non-personalised ads or none.
  • Anywhere, you can reset or delete your advertising ID, and opt out of ad personalisation, in Android's settings under Privacy → Ads. With the advertising ID deleted, ads are non-personalised.

Our server never receives the advertising ID or anything else from the ads SDK; we see only aggregate reports from Google about ad impressions and earnings.

6. In-app purchases through Google Play

Locked lines and the ad removal can be bought through Google Play's billing system. Payment is handled entirely by Google; the app never sees your payment details. Google tells the app which products your Google account owns so that a purchase can be restored, including after you reinstall the app or change phones. Purchase records are kept by Google under the Google Privacy Policy and the Google Play Terms of Service.

7. Information we do not collect

  • No account, and no name other than the board name you choose.
  • No email address, phone number, contacts, location, photos, camera, or microphone.
  • Our server does not receive device identifiers such as the Android ID or the advertising ID; the advertising ID is used only by Google's advertising SDK, as described in section 5.
  • Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared with anyone beyond the services named in this policy: Amazon Web Services, which hosts our server, and Google (Play, AdMob, Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Cloud Messaging).

The Android permissions the app uses are vibrate, for the click of the master controller's notches; Google Play billing, for in-app purchases; internet, for our server and the ads; notifications, asked for only when you switch alerts on; and network state and the advertising ID, which Google's advertising SDK needs to request and show ads.

8. Where data is kept and for how long

  • Data saved by the app stays in the app's private storage on your phone, which other apps cannot read, until you clear the app's data in Android settings or uninstall the app.
  • Run reports, usage events and board rows are stored on Amazon Web Services in the United States and sent over HTTPS. Rows on the day boards expire after 90 days and rows on the week boards after 2 years; all-time board rows and run reports are kept while your player record exists. Copies of run reports and usage events in our statistics archive are kept indefinitely, marked with the player ID only (see section 9).
  • Push tokens are kept only while alerts are on.
  • Crash reports are kept by Firebase for 90 days. Data held by Google for ads, purchases and messaging is retained according to Google's policies.

9. Deleting your data

Open the logbook from the title board and tap DELETE MY DATA. The server removes your player record, your name, your push token, your run reports and your rows on every world board, and the app wipes its records on the phone. This cannot be undone. Copies of run reports and usage events kept in our statistics archive stay there, marked only with the player ID; because the player record and name are gone, nothing links them to you or to your phone any longer. Lines and the ad removal you have bought are restored from Google Play. Uninstalling the app without deleting first leaves the anonymous run reports on the server; email us (section 14) with your board name and we will remove them.

11. Children

The app is a general-audience game and is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. Ads are requested from Google as described in section 5. Google requires an account of a qualifying age to make purchases; those age rules are Google's.

12. Your rights

You can delete everything the server holds about you at any time with DELETE MY DATA (section 9), and you can see your own board entries and run history in the app. Because we hold no name, email address or account for you, we cannot look up your data from a request alone; if you email us with your board name we will find and remove your records. Progress on your phone is under your control through Android's app settings. Purchase history is under your control through your Google account, and advertising choices through the PRIVACY chip and Android's ad settings. Depending on where you live you may have further rights, such as access, correction, portability or objection; contact us and we will respond.

13. Changes to this policy

If the app's data handling changes, for example if a future version sends data anywhere new, we will update this page and change the effective date at the top. Continued use of the app after a change means you accept the updated policy.
